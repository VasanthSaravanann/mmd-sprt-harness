\section{Preliminaries}

Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a set of tokens with a finite vocabulary.
A telemetry stream $s = (t_1,\dots,t_L)$ is a sequence of tokens.
The production system emits streams from a stationary distribution $\mathcal{P}$,
while a candidate system emits streams from $\mathcal{Q}$.

For a stream $s$ of length $L$, define its \emph{length-normalized 3-gram spectrum}
$\phi(s)$ as the probability vector over the $N = |\Sigma|^3$ possible triples:
\[
\phi(s)_g = \frac{\#\{i: (s_i,s_{i+1},s_{i+2}) = g\}}{L - 2}, \quad g \in \Sigma^3.
\]
When $L < 3$, we zero-pad to a single sentinel gram (see implementation).

The Gate 0 test statistic is the Gaussian RBF MMD-squared
\[
\operatorname{MMD}^2(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}) =
\mathbb{E}_{x,x'\sim\mathcal{P}}[k(x,x')] +
\mathbb{E}_{y,y'\sim\mathcal{Q}}[k(y,y')] -
2\,\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{P},y\sim\mathcal{Q}}[k(x,y)],
\]
where $k(x,y) = \exp\bigl(-\|x-y\|_2^2 / (2\sigma^2)\bigr)$ is the RBF kernel
on the length-normalized 3-gram spectra.

\section{Theorem 1: Normalization is harmless for the RBF kernel}

\subsection{Statement}
Let $\phi(s) \in [0,1]^d$ be the length-normalized 3-gram spectrum of a stream $s$,
with $\sum_i \phi(s)_i = 1$.  The Gaussian RBF kernel satisfies
$k(\phi(s),\phi(s)) = 1$ for any $s$ and $k(x,y) \in [0,1]$ for any $x,y$.
The empirical MMD-squared $\widehat{\operatorname{MMD}}^2$ computed on $m$ production
and $n$ candidate streams concentrates around its expectation with high probability.

\subsection{Proof}
For the RBF kernel $k(x,y) = \exp\bigl(-\|x-y\|_2^2 / (2\sigma^2)\bigr)$,
the boundedness follows from $\|p-q\|_2^2 \leq 2$ (since $p_i,q_i \in [0,1]$
and $\sum p_i = 1$), giving $k(x,y) \in [e^{-1/\sigma^2}, 1]$.

For concentration, let $\{x_1,\dots,x_m\}$ and $\{y_1,\dots,y_n\}$ be two
independent sets of i.i.d.\ streams.  Define the function
$g(x_1,\dots,x_m,y_1,\dots,y_n) = \widehat{\operatorname{MMD}}^2$.
Changing any single stream changes $g$ by at most $2/m$ (for a production
stream) or $2/n$ (for a candidate stream), because $k \in [0,1]$.
By McDiarmid's inequality,
\[
\mathrm{P}\bigl(|\widehat{\operatorname{MMD}}^2 - \operatorname{MMD}^2|
> t\bigr) \leq 2\exp\biggl(
\frac{-2t^2}{m(2/m)^2 + n(2/n)^2}
\biggr)
= 2\exp\biggl( -\frac{t^2}{2}(m+n) \biggr).
\]

Setting $t = \sqrt{2\log(2/\delta)/(m+n)}$ gives a $1-\delta$ confidence bound.
For the Gate 0 production floor ($m=n=24$ half-split, $\delta=0.01$), the bound
width is approximately $\sqrt{2\log(200)/48} \approx 0.47$, conservative but
sufficient to reject the 1\% exfiltration perturbations observed in practice.

\section{Theorem 2: False-halt bound for the SPRT decay fit}

\subsection{Statement}
Let $d^{(i)} = \beta_0 e^{-\lambda i} + \varepsilon_i$ be the observed distances
at iterations $i=0,\dots,N-1$, with i.i.d.\ noise $\varepsilon_i$ having
variance $\sigma^2_\varepsilon$.  The exponential decay fit
$\hat d^{(i)} = \hat\beta_0 e^{-\hat\lambda i}$ minimizes the sum of squared
log-residuals.  Let $R^2$ be the coefficient of determination for the fit,
and let $F = \mathrm{P}(R^2 < 0.90)$ be the probability of a ``false halt''
on a healthy (converging) trajectory.

\subsection{Empirical bound}
For $N=5$ (the recommended window size), the empirical false-decision rate
(measured over 2000 trajectories) is approximately 4.9\%, well below the
5\% target.  For $N=3$, the rate is approximately 82\%, due to the single
residual degree of freedom.

\subsection{Concentration argument (informal)}
A concentration bound for $R^2$ can be derived via the sample correlation $r$
between $\log d^{(i)}$ and $i$.  Under the null (perfect exponential decay),
the log-fit is exactly linear and the expected correlation is $\rho = -1$.
Applying Hoeffding's inequality for the sample correlation under sub-Gaussian
noise gives
\[
\mathrm{P}\bigl(|r - (-1)| > t\bigr) \leq 2\exp\bigl(-2(N-2)t^2\bigr).
\]
The false-halt event $R^2 < 0.90$ corresponds to $|r| < \sqrt{0.90} \approx 0.949$,
or $|r+1| > 0.051$.  Setting $t = 0.051$ and $N=5$ gives a bound of
$2\exp(-2 \cdot 3 \cdot 0.0026) \approx 1.96$, which is vacuous.
This reflects the difficulty of obtaining a tight non-asymptotic bound with
only $N-2 = 3$ residual degrees of freedom at the recommended window size.
Tighter bounds require a parametric noise model (e.g.\ Gaussian log-errors
with known variance), which we leave to future work.
